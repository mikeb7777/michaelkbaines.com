About

“The methods don’t change. The application does.”Michael K. Baines

I spent 39 years in engineering. My career began in refrigeration systems, moved to aircraft refrigeration, and then to the other systems that keep an aircraft in the air. At Boeing I taught Designer Development, the program that trains designers and engineers in Boeing’s parts systems, and I designed a test parachute system for the F-22. I designed engines at Honeywell, worked on business jets for Dassault Falcon Jet and Gulfstream, spent ten years at Bombardier on the Global business jets and the clean-sheet C Series regional jet, and worked as a structural flight test engineer on the certification program for Mitsubishi’s regional jet. Later I taught English at a university in Thailand.

Aerospace engineering teaches one habit for life: nothing is trusted until it has been checked, and checked again. I took that habit to bigger questions. The physics became A Quest for The Big TOE and the Ic² Research Institute, where predictions are registered before the data arrive and every result is published. The human question became Rediscovering Oneness.
